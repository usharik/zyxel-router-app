Router Manager for Zyxel – Privacy Policy

Application: Android, package com.usharik.zyxelrouter
Developer: Alexey Usharik (“we”, “us”)
Effective date: 13 November 2026 · Last updated: 14 November 2026

Summary
  • The App talks directly to the router on your local network. It has no server of its own and creates no accounts.
  • Your router password is stored only on your device, encrypted with an Android Keystore key, and only if you choose “Remember password”.
  • Information read from the router (device names, IP and MAC addresses, Wi‑Fi network names, parental-control profiles, the system log) stays on your device. Only the router model, its firmware version and counts (such as the number of connected devices) are included in analytics (section 3.4); addresses, names and passwords are never sent to us or to Google services. Only what you share yourself leaves the device.
  • To identify devices, the App uses a built-in manufacturer registry and asks devices on your home network to describe themselves. What they answer stays on your phone (section 2.8).
  • A Wi‑Fi password is read from the router only when you ask to share that network. It is never saved and is sent only to the app you pick in the Android share menu.
  • The demo mode works on built-in sample data without connecting to any router.
  • Release builds show Google AdMob ads (a banner and occasional full-screen ads). An optional one-time Google Play purchase removes ads.
  • Release builds use Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports) and Firebase Analytics (limited usage events without addresses or names). In the EEA and the UK they are turned on only if you consent.

1. Scope

This policy describes how the Router Manager for Zyxel Android app (the “App”) handles information. The App is an unofficial third-party utility and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Zyxel Networks Corporation.

2. Information the App handles on your device

2.1 Router address and credentials

You enter the router’s local IP address, user name and password. The address and user name are stored in the App’s private storage. The password is stored only if you enable “Remember password”; it is encrypted with a key kept in the Android Keystore. Logging out deletes the saved password. The App sends the credentials only to the router address you entered, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection.

2.2 Router certificate fingerprint

Routers use self-signed certificates. On the first connection the App remembers the SHA-256 fingerprint of the router’s certificate and later refuses to connect if it changes, unless you confirm the new certificate. The fingerprint is stored locally.

2.3 Data read from the router

To show its screens the App reads from the router: connection status, external IP address, connected devices (names, IP and MAC addresses, traffic counters, Wi‑Fi signal), Wi‑Fi network names and settings, parental-control profiles, routes and the router’s system log. Changes you make (parental-control profiles, reboot) are sent only to the router.

2.4 Sharing a Wi‑Fi network

When you choose “Share network” for a Wi‑Fi network (including a guest network), the App reads that network’s password from the router at that moment. Wi‑Fi passwords are not read otherwise. The App shows a QR code for joining the network; the password itself stays hidden until you choose to show it. If you copy the password, it is placed on the clipboard marked as sensitive, so Android does not display it in clipboard previews. If you tap “Share”, the App passes a PNG image of the QR code and a text with the network name and password to the Android share menu, and they go only to the app you pick there. The PNG image is kept temporarily in the App’s private cache and deleted when you close the window. The password is never saved by the App and is never sent to us or to Google services.

2.5 Demo mode

“Try without a router” on the login screen opens the App with built-in sample data. In this mode the App does not connect to any router and does not read, store or send router data or credentials.

2.6 Device history

The App keeps a local list of devices it has seen (MAC address, name, type, connection and time last seen, up to 200 per router), so that devices the router has forgotten can still be shown and added to parental-control profiles. You can remove a device from this list with “Forget”. Uninstalling the App deletes it.

2.7 Ad and offer frequency

To limit how often ads and the ad removal offer appear, the App stores locally when it was first launched, how many times it has connected to the router, how many changes were saved since the last full-screen ad, and when the last full-screen ad, ad removal offer and rating request were shown.

2.8 Identifying devices

To tell what an unknown device is, the App looks up the manufacturer of its MAC address in a copy of the public IEEE registry built into the App, without any network request. A MAC address that the device chose at random (as phones and laptops do for privacy) is shown as a “private Wi‑Fi address”.

When you open the device list in a session, and when you tap “Identify”, the App asks devices on your home network to describe themselves using standard local protocols: it sends mDNS/Bonjour and SSDP/UPnP discovery queries on the local Wi‑Fi or Ethernet network and NetBIOS name queries to the private addresses the router listed. For devices that answer SSDP it downloads their UPnP description (a small XML file) over HTTP from the same private address that answered. The App does not scan ports and does not contact anything outside your local network for this.

What devices answer (their names, network names, manufacturer, model, workgroup and the services they offer) is stored only in the App’s private storage, linked to the device’s MAC address and to the router address you signed in to, so it stays available when the device is offline or after you sign in again. It never leaves your phone. “Forget” removes it together with the device history; clearing the App’s data or uninstalling the App deletes all of it.

3. Third-party services

3.1 Google AdMob

Release builds show an anchored banner at the bottom of the main screens (not on the login screen and not in landscape on phones) and occasionally a full-screen ad at a natural pause: after the router starts rebooting, or after several saved parental-control changes. There are no full-screen ads during the first 24 hours after installation, and at most one every 20 minutes. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the App first asks for your consent with Google’s consent form; you can change your choice later from the App menu. The Google Mobile Ads SDK may process advertising or app-instance identifiers, IP address, device and operating-system information, approximate location derived from the IP address, and ad interactions, to deliver, limit, personalise (if you consent) and measure ads. The App does not give AdMob any information read from your router. See Google’s partner information and Privacy Policy.

3.2 When Crashlytics and Analytics are turned on

Crashlytics and Analytics stay off until the App has received an answer to Google’s consent form. In the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom they are turned on only if you consent to storing and accessing information on your device (purpose 1 of the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework); otherwise they stay off. Outside these regions they are turned on right away. If you have bought “Remove ads”, the consent form is not shown, and Crashlytics and Analytics are turned on only outside the EEA and the UK. Where Google’s consent form applies, you can change your choice at any time from the App menu (section 8).

3.3 Firebase Crashlytics

When the App crashes or hits an unexpected error, Crashlytics receives a technical report: stack trace, device model, Android version, App version, the router model and firmware version, and the name of the failed operation. IP and MAC addresses are removed from error messages before sending.

3.4 Firebase Analytics

The App sends limited usage events: which screen was opened, successful or failed login with a category (for example “invalid credentials” or “unreachable”), number of connected devices, parental-control profile saved or deleted (with the number of devices and days, not their names), router reboot, a Wi‑Fi QR code shown (wifi_qr_shown, with only whether it is a guest network) or shared (wifi_qr_shared), how many devices answered the local-network discovery (devices_discovered, a number only), ad and purchase events, and the router model and firmware. It never sends router addresses, device names, MAC addresses, Wi‑Fi names or passwords, credentials or log contents. Collection of the advertising ID and ad personalisation signals by Analytics is disabled. Firebase may associate events with an app-instance identifier. Debug builds send nothing.

3.5 Google Play Billing

The optional “Remove ads” purchase is processed by Google Play. The App receives your purchases of this App (product, state, purchase token) and uses them only to turn ads off and to acknowledge the purchase; it stores a single “ads removed” flag on the device. Payment details are never available to the App or to us.

3.6 Google Play In-App Review

After several successful sessions the App may ask Google Play to show its rating card, at most once per App version and once every 90 days. Your rating goes directly to Google Play; the App does not learn it.

4. Data sharing and sale

We do not sell personal data. Apart from the Google services above, the App sends data only to your router, sends discovery queries to devices on your local network (section 2.8) and, when you share a Wi‑Fi network, to the app you pick in the share menu (section 2.4).

5. Security

Connections to the router use HTTPS with certificate pinning (section 2.2); the router’s login is additionally encrypted as its web interface requires. Connections to Google services use TLS.

6. Retention and deletion

Data stored on your device remains until you log out (password), forget a device (device history and what the device reported about itself), clear the App’s data or uninstall the App. The QR code image for sharing a Wi‑Fi network is deleted when you close the sharing window. Crashlytics and Analytics data is retained by Google according to the Firebase retention settings (at most 14 months for Analytics, 90 days for Crashlytics).

7. Children

The App is a network administration tool and is not directed to children under 13.

8. Your choices

  • Change consent for ads, Crashlytics and Analytics at any time: App menu → “Ad and privacy settings” (shown where required).
  • Remove ads with the one-time purchase.
  • Reset your Android advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads in the Android settings.

9. Changes and contact

We may update this policy when the App changes. The current version is always at https://usharik.github.io/zyxelrouter/privacy-policy.html.
